Booking Desk Runbook

Green Vital Travel & Tour Agency Ltd.
Freetown, Sierra Leone

One page, start to finish: what happens from the moment someone reaches out, to the moment they're home and asked for a review. Meant to survive being busy, sick, or eventually handing part of this to someone else.

The Process

8 stages
01

Inquiry lands

Comes in through the contact form, the site chatbot handing off, WhatsApp, or a direct call/email. Website submissions arrive by email with a reference code already attached (format MR-XXXXXX for bookings) — nothing needs to be re-typed to start tracking it.

Owner: whoever checks the inbox first
02

First reply

Acknowledge same day if at all possible. If dates, group size, or passport nationality weren't given, ask now — the next two stages can't start without them.

Target: same business day
03

Verify entry requirements

Before quoting anything — check the destination's actual embassy/consular site for the client's specific passport. Requirements shift with little notice and this is the check that saves a trip from unravelling at the gate.

Never guess — official source only
04

Draft the itinerary

For an existing package, adapt it to the brief. For anything custom, the internal drafting tool gives a first-pass structure in seconds — real weather data and real Green Vital package data baked in, zero pricing. Edit it like any other draft; it's a starting point, not a send-ready document.

Tool: /tools/itinerary
05

Send the real quote

Pricing, what's included/excluded, deposit amount, and cancellation terms — added by a person, from current numbers. This is the one stage nothing automated ever touches.

Standing rule: the chatbot and the itinerary tool are both hard-coded to never state a price. If either ever appears to quote a number, that's a bug — pricing comes from a human, every time, no exceptions.
06

Confirm & deposit

Client accepts, deposit is collected, reference code gets logged as booked rather than just inquired. This is the moment a lead should move from "someone to follow up with" to "someone with a real trip."

07

Pre-trip & on-trip

Confirm supplier bookings (flights, hotel, transfers) ahead of departure; send the final itinerary and a documents checklist a set number of days out. During the trip, one number to call — a delayed flight or a changed booking shouldn't become the story of the trip.

08

After they're home

Ask for a review. Every submitted review gets checked against a real booking before it's published — never published automatically, by design.

/reviews

Keeping Track

Right now this lives in an inbox — fine at low volume, easy to lose a lead in as it grows. The minimum worth having: one running list (a spreadsheet is enough to start) with name · destination · status · reference code · last contacted. The status column is what actually matters — new → quoted → booked → completed → lost — since "who did I promise to follow up with this week" is the question an inbox can't answer at a glance.
